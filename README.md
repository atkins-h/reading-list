## Completed
### Non-Fiction

- *The Kill Chain: Defending America in the Future of High-Tech Warfare* — Christian Brose
> Recommended -  Written in 2020, it well predicted the state of the battlefield today. Must read for people interested in modern warfare.
- *Counter-Intelligence: What the Secret World Can Teach Us About Problem-Solving and Creativity* — Robert Hannigan
> Not Recommended - interest read although misses the mark by occasionally crossing into victimhood mentality. There is limited value in complaining about the wrongs perpetrated on brilliant individuals in the past if you don't celebrate what they were good at.
- *The Dragons and the Snakes: How the Rest Learned to Fight the West* — David Kilcullen
> Recommended - good description of the pressures of adaptation and how sometimes strategic actions can breed worse enemies. Necessary reading if attempting to understand how Australia could generate asymmetric advantage. must read for those interested in irregular warfare
- *The Strategy Dialogues: A Primer on Business Strategy and Strategic Management* — John Hillen
> Recommended - Strange presentation and format but potentially a good communication tool. Attempts to help those not strategically minded understand through worked example
- *12 Seconds Of Silence: How a Team of Inventors, Tinkerers, and Spies Took Down a Nazi Superweapon* — Jamie Holmes
> Highly Recommended - Great story, aspiration, great model of how to run R&D
- *Prisoners of Geography: Ten Maps That Tell You Everything You Need to Know About Global Politics* — Tim Marshall
> Highly Recommended - Critics are quick to argue that Tim Marshall oversimplifies nuanced politics into basic geography but this is foundational knowledge irrespective of whether all opinions are bang on.
- *Dark Wire: The Incredible True Story of the Largest Sting Operation Ever* — Joseph Cox
> Not recommended - Could have been good but tried to make a true crime thriller rather than letting the interesting facts speak for themselves
- *Conflict: The Evolution of Warfare from 1945 to Ukraine* — David H. Petraeus & Andrew Roberts
> Not Recommended - The book is interesting but primarily written through the egotistical lens of a 4 star general. It *is* interesting to get an insight into the mind of Petraeus.
- *The Cuckoo’s Egg: Tracking a Spy Through the Maze of Computer Espionage* — Clifford Stoll
> Recommended - Entertaining, fascinating insight into how the internet got started
- *Pegasus: How a Spy in Your Pocket Threatens Global Security* — Laurent Richard & Sandrine Rigaud
> Not Recommended - interesting enough but centered around the investigation and the investigators rather than the subject of investigation. self indulgent
- *Grokking Algorithms: An Illustrated Guide for Programmers and Other Curious People* — Aditya Y. Bhargava
> Recommended - good foundational knowledge if you're a non programmmer
- *The Spy and the Traitor: The Greatest Espionage Story of the Cold War* — Ben Macintyre
> Recommended - entertaining
- *Agent Zigzag: The Most Notorious Double Agent of World War II* — Ben Macintyre
> Recommended - entertaining
- *Chip War: The Fight for the World's Most Critical Technology* — Chris Miller
> Highly Recommended -  A must read for anyone interesting in one of the driving factors in the current US/China great power competition
- *Humble Pi: A Comedy of Maths Errors* — Matt Parker
> Recommended - great entertaining light read
- *The Prince* - Niccolò Machiavelli
> Recommended - Basics of human nature. Allows the reader to understand the tenets of political power and how they haven't changed over the years. Strict implementation could be interesting
- *The Art of War* - Sun Tzu
> Classic reading - Interesting, however not as applicable as other books to modern life. General principles *could* be extrapolated if required or desired.
- *Calculus made easy* - Silvanus P Thompson
> Highly Recommended - Written in 1910. The author takes ordinary problems and demonstrates how difficult it is trying to solve them with conventional maths. The concepts of calculus are introduced only once existing methods have been tried, allowing the reader to truly understand the intent behind the method and how it might apply to real problems
- *Sandworm: A New Era of Cyberwar and the Hunt for the Kremlin's Most Dangerous Hackers* - Andy Greenberg
> Highly Recommended - Many lessons to be learned. Will be a playbook for future competition
- *Failure is not an option* - Gene Kranz
> Highly Recommended - provides an insight into one of the greatest achievements of American engineering and science. Gives a view into how some of the highest performing teams in the world function. Entertaining
- *No Worse Enemy: The Inside Story of the Chaotic Struggle for Afghanistan* - Ben Anderson
> Highly Recommended - excellent story telling, matches the youtube series well. 
- *The Operators The Wild and Terrifying Inside Story of America's War in Afghanistan* - Michael Hastings
> Recommended - Excellent and critical insight into the poitical machinations behind the American war effort in Afghanistan. No longer available to buy. Modern day burning of the books
- *Longitude: The True Story of a Lone Genius Who Solved the Greatest Scientific Problem of His Time* - Dava Sobel
> Recommended - good read, interesting background on the problem, the journey to solve it, and all of the associated stories. Good foundational knowledge for those interested in geospatial concepts
- *Skunk Works: A Personal Memoir of My Years at Lockheed* - Ben R. Rich, Leo Janos
> Highly Recommend - Aspirational, it depicts an engineers paradise. If only all engineering organisations could learn to function this way. The depiction of flawed but otherwise brilliant characters allows you to understand the need to tolerate eccentricity and personality quirks.
